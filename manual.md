This Operations Manual serves as the comprehensive pass-down guide for the Community Risk Reduction (CRR) Officer position at the Denton Fire Department. It is designed to provide a successor with everything needed to assume and effectively manage the full scope of this position, which encompasses both Community Risk Reduction/Public Education and Firefighter Recruitment operations.

The CRR Officer role is unique within the department: a single full-time equivalent (FTE) responsible for 31 distinct program areas spanning community outreach, public education, technology development, the entire Civil Service hiring process, accreditation support, pension board service, and administrative reporting. This manual captures the institutional knowledge, standard operating procedures, vendor relationships, technology systems, and strategic context necessary to maintain continuity across all of these functions.

# **Chapter 1: Denton Fire Department Overview**

## 1.1 Department History and Mission

The Denton Fire Department (DFD) was founded in 1874 and has served the City of Denton continuously for over 150 years. Today, DFD provides an all-hazards approach model that includes response to fires, emergency medical services (EMS), hazardous materials response, and technical rescues. The department serves a population of approximately 166,000 residents across the City of Denton, Texas.

**Mission Statement:** The Denton Fire Department is a team of dedicated professionals who serve to protect lives and property in our community.

**Vision Statement:** The Denton Fire Department will be an innovative leader that proactively embraces the future while honoring the traditions of the fire service.

### RICH Core Values

The Denton Fire Department operates under the RICH values framework, which serves as the organizational pillars:

- Respect: Treating all individuals with dignity and consideration
- Integrity: Maintaining honesty and strong moral principles in all actions
- Courage: Demonstrating bravery in the face of adversity, both on and off the fireground
- Humility: Serving others before self and maintaining a learner's mindset

## 1.2 Department Organization

The Denton Fire Department is led by Fire Chief Kenneth Hedges and operates from nine fire stations strategically positioned throughout the city. The department employs approximately 235 sworn personnel and support staff organized across several divisions.

## 1.3 Accreditations and Distinctions

The Denton Fire Department holds several significant national accreditations and ratings that reflect the quality of services provided:

### ISO Class 1 Rating

Effective October 2025, the Denton Fire Department achieved an ISO (Insurance Services Office) Class 1 rating, placing it among the top 1% of fire departments nationwide. This rating directly impacts homeowner insurance premiums for Denton residents and reflects the department's commitment to excellence in fire suppression capability, emergency communications, water supply, and community risk reduction.

### CAAS Accreditation

In August 2025, DFD achieved perfect-score accreditation from the Commission on Accreditation of Ambulance Services (CAAS), making it one of only 34 departments in the United States and Canada to hold this distinction. CAAS accreditation demonstrates compliance with rigorous standards for clinical quality, response time performance, and operational excellence in emergency medical services.

### CPSE/CFAI Accreditation

The department participates in the Commission on Fire Accreditation International (CFAI) through the Commission on Professional Standards for Excellence (CPSE). This accreditation process involves comprehensive self-assessment, community risk assessment, and Standards of Cover documentation. The CRR Officer plays a direct role in supporting accreditation through program appraisals, data collection, and recommendation responses. (See Chapter 14 for detailed accreditation responsibilities.)

## 1.4 Department Programs Relevant to CRR

The CRR Officer interacts with or supports several department programs beyond the core CRR and Recruitment functions:

### Emergency Management

The City of Denton Emergency Management division (Bob Martinez, Emergency Manager; Alyssa Owen, Deputy) coordinates the city's emergency management program across all four phases: Preparedness, Response, Recovery, and Mitigation. Emergency Management offers free community training including Prepare Denton, Stop the Bleed, and Active Threat Response. The CRR Officer should coordinate with Emergency Management on overlapping community outreach efforts and cross-promote training opportunities through CRR newsletters.

### Fire Prevention / Fire Marshal's Office

The Fire Prevention division handles fire code enforcement, plan review, inspections, and fire investigations. Key systems include eTRAKiT for permits and inspections (no paper submittals accepted), The Compliance Engine for fire protection system deficiency tracking, and the requirement for 3rd party plan review on fire construction permits effective November 2024. Fire Prevention staff are recipients of Water Shutoff Survey notifications for sprinkler system shutoffs. The CRR Officer maintains the Fire Watch Guidelines tool that supports Fire Prevention operations.

### Free Smoke Detector Program

DFD has partnered with the American Red Cross to provide and install free smoke detectors within Denton city limits. Community members can complete an online form to request free detectors. In Q1 FY 25-26, the CRR Officer coordinated a smoke detector installation drive resulting in 50 detectors installed in homes that previously lacked working smoke detection. This program should be promoted through CRR newsletters and community events.

### Community Wildfire Protection Plan (CWPP)

The Denton County CWPP is a multi-agency effort to identify areas with higher wildfire risk and develop mitigation strategies. While primarily managed through the Fire Marshal's Office, the CRR Officer should be aware of this plan and incorporate wildfire preparedness messaging into seasonal newsletters when appropriate.

# **Chapter 2: Position Overview**

## 2.1 Position Summary

The Community Risk Reduction Officer is a single-FTE position responsible for the full scope of both Community Risk Reduction/Public Education and Firefighter Recruitment/Hiring at the Denton Fire Department. This dual-mission role is unique within the department and carries sole responsibility for 31 distinct program areas spanning community outreach, public education delivery, newsletter production, technology tool development, the entire Civil Service hiring process, accreditation support, and administrative reporting.

The position also carries additional duties as a Pension Board Trustee for the Denton Firemen's Relief and Retirement Fund (~$185M AUM), including Real Estate Sub-Committee membership, and must maintain personal firefighter/paramedic continuing education requirements (TCFP/DSHS certifications).

## 2.2 Primary Responsibilities

### Community Risk Reduction (CRR)

- Plan, coordinate, and deliver public education events (fire safety presentations, fire truck displays, station tours, fire extinguisher training)
- Produce and distribute monthly CRR newsletters via Constant Contact
- Develop and maintain web-based CRR operational tools (Water Shutoff Survey, Fire Watch Guidelines, Knox Box Guide, After the Fire)
- Manage special programs: Citizens Fire Academy, Explorer/Scout Program, Clown Program, Fire Camp
- Track and report community outreach metrics through ESO system
- Coordinate with Fire Prevention, Emergency Management, and Operations on overlapping programs
- Support CPSE/CAAS accreditation through program appraisals and recommendation responses

### Firefighter Recruitment & Hiring

- Generate annual Position Opportunity Announcement (POA) for Civil Service Commission approval
- Coordinate all Civil Service exam logistics: test provider (FPSI), venue, materials, proctors
- Manage the full NEOGOV hiring workflow from application through appointment
- Verify candidate extra points (military service, residency, LaGrone Academy)
- Certify eligibility list and present demographics report to Civil Service Commission
- Coordinate each hiring stage: Personal History Statement, polygraph (VerifEYE), PAT, panel interviews, psych exam, medical exam
- Generate all correspondence: disqualification letters, conditional letters of employment, appointment letters
- Produce and distribute recruiting newsletters ("Ready to Serve" series)
- Conduct recruiting events at partner institutions (UNT, TWU, high schools, fire academies)
- Manage diversity recruitment initiatives

### Administrative & Reporting

- Submit weekly task reports to Fire Chief (auto-generated via Power Automate on Sundays)
- Prepare quarterly Program Appraisals with full metrics, budget reconciliation, and appendices
- Manage P-Card reconciliation and expense tracking across four budget accounts
- Attend Monday Staff Meetings, CRR Meetings, Command Staff meetings, and Civil Service Commission meetings

# **Chapter 3: Key Relationships and Partners**

## 3.1 Internal Stakeholders

### Fire Chief's Office

The CRR Officer submits weekly task reports directly to Chief Hedges. Quarterly Program Appraisals are also routed through the Chief's office. The Chief's office provides strategic direction on department priorities that affect CRR and recruitment messaging. The hiring and termination report is prepared for the Chief as needed.

### Division Chief (Chief Skipper)

Chief Skipper provides direct operational oversight for CRR activities. Regular CRA (Community Risk Assessment) meetings are held to align CRR programs with identified community risks. The CRR Officer coordinates with Chief Skipper on interview process development, UAS program direction, and CRR strategic priorities.

### Fire Prevention Division

Fire Prevention receives automated notifications from the Water Shutoff Survey when sprinkler system shutoffs are reported. The CRR Officer maintains the Fire Watch Guidelines tool that supports Fire Prevention operations during fire protection system impairments. Coordinate with Fire Prevention on After the Fire resources and building safety information (see the Building Safety reconstruction steps document).

### Operations Division

On-duty Operations crews support CRR events when available within their districts, though dedicated overtime for CRR activities was discontinued due to budget constraints in 2025. Operations personnel use the Water Shutoff Survey tool in the field and should be trained on its use. The CRR Officer coordinates with specific companies for PAT preparation help (e.g., Truck 1 for Practice PAT support).

### Civil Service Commission

The CRR Officer provides deliverables to the Civil Service Commission including the Position Opportunity Announcement, certified eligibility lists, demographics reports, and communications about upcoming meetings. Commission meetings require attendance and prepared materials.

### MarComm (Marketing & Communications)

DFD MarComm supports CRR and Recruiting through collaborative content creation (Veterans Day campaigns, video script development), social media coordination, and brand consistency. The CRR Officer coordinates with MarComm for major campaigns and media inquiry responses.

## 3.2 External Partners

### University of North Texas (UNT)

UNT is the primary university recruitment partner. The CRR Officer maintains a Handshake account for posting positions and events. Key programs include ELEVATE Athlete recruiting events, career fairs, and campus presentations. UNT has also been a partner for media content (e.g., battery safety interview). Maintain regular contact with UNT career services and the ELEVATE program coordinator.

### Texas Woman's University (TWU)

TWU is a secondary recruitment partner. The CRR Officer attends TWU career fairs and recruitment events. Coordinate with TWU career services for event scheduling.

### Local High Schools

Career day presentations are conducted at local high schools including Guyer High School, Calhoun Middle School, and Strickland Middle School. These events introduce students to fire service careers and serve both CRR education and long-term recruitment pipeline development.

### Fire Academies

Recruit from fire academies including LaGrone Advanced Technology Center (whose graduates receive +1 preference point on the Civil Service exam), North Central Texas College (NCTC), and Weatherford College. Academy visits should be scheduled quarterly during active recruiting cycles.

### FPSI (Fire & Police Selection, Inc.)

FPSI is the contracted vendor for Civil Service exam administration. They provide test materials (booklets), scoring, and study guide resources. The CRR Officer is responsible for executing the FPSI contract, coordinating exam logistics, and ensuring timely return of test booklets after each exam. Study guides are available through firepolicejobs.com.

### VerifEYE

VerifEYE was selected in FY 25-26 as the replacement polygraph vendor, offering remote testing capability through a mobile app version, enhanced accuracy, and cost savings compared to traditional polygraph methods. The CRR Officer coordinates onboarding, scheduling, and contract management with VerifEYE.

### Scouting America (formerly Scouting America)

The Fire Explorer program is managed in coordination with Scouting America. Annual dues ($66.95 per instructor), scout safety training (3 hours), and membership renewals are managed by the CRR Officer. The Explorer program was successfully renewed in Q1 FY 25-26.

### Constant Contact

The email marketing platform used for both CRR and Recruiting newsletters. The CRR Officer manages subscriber lists, creates newsletter content, and tracks analytics (open rates, click-through rates, unsubscribe rates). As of September 30, 2026, the Frontline Report (CRR) reaches 536 subscribers and Ready to Serve (Recruiting) reaches 5,186.

# **Chapter 4: CRR Strategy and Program Framework**

## 4.1 Strategic Alignment

Community Risk Reduction programs at DFD are directly aligned with the Department Strategic Plan. Two goals specifically govern CRR operations:

**Goal 3:** Develop Community Risk Reduction Plan following NFPA 1300: Align CRR programs with national standards, document risk assessment methodology, and track intervention effectiveness.

**Goal 4:** Conduct thorough review of Public Education programs in line with Standards of Cover/CPSE. Evaluate program reach and effectiveness, document outcomes and metrics, and maintain a continuous improvement cycle.

**Division Objectives:** Evaluate and analyze data to reduce risk in the community through education. Recruit and retain a diverse workforce.

## 4.2 Target Hazard-Based Approach

Beginning in FY 25-26, the CRR program implemented a data-driven approach to public education programming. All content is directly tied to identified community hazards through two primary sources:

- Incident investigation data from the Fire Marshal’s Office
- Nationally recognized seasonal and emerging hazard trends

This approach ensures that every piece of public education content addresses real, documented risks rather than generic safety messaging. The monthly CRR Newsletter program was specifically designed around this target hazard analysis framework.

## 5.6 Hoarding Awareness Campaign

In January-February 2025, the CRR program produced a public awareness video following a series of hoarding-related fires and fire fatalities in the community. The video featured a multi-disciplinary perspective:

- CRR Officer discussing the scope of the problem (hoarding contributes to approximately 25% of all preventable fire deaths)
- Truck Captain addressing rescue complications
- Engine Lieutenant explaining increased fire load and suppression challenges
- Firefighter/Paramedic covering medical emergency access barriers

The campaign included a call to action for the department’s Free Home Safety Inspection program, directly connecting public education to an intervention service. This campaign demonstrates the department’s expansion beyond traditional fire safety topics into non-traditional risk areas.

## 6.3 Newsletter Production Workflow

1. Topic selection: Review incident data and seasonal hazard calendar. Identify the primary risk topic for the month.
1. Content development: Research the topic, gather statistics (national and local), develop 3-5 actionable safety tips.
1. Draft in Constant Contact: Follow the standard template structure (see below). Include DFD branding and images.
1. Review: Proofread for accuracy, check all links, verify statistics.
1. Send: Schedule or send the newsletter. Typical send day: first week of the month.
1. Track analytics: After 48 hours, review open rates, click-through rates, and engagement.
1. Archive link: Save the Constant Contact archive URL for quarterly report appendix.

### Standard Newsletter Structure

- Opening hook: Seasonal or timely connection to grab reader attention
- Main safety topic: 3-5 specific, actionable recommendations
- Supporting statistics: National data to contextualize the risk
- DFD resources mention: Link to relevant tools, programs, or services
- Call to action: Share the newsletter, contact DFD, attend an event, or access a resource

## 7.3 Fire Watch Guidelines

The Fire Watch Guidelines tool provides interactive guidance for managing fire protection system impairments per International Fire Code (IFC) requirements. It includes three digital fillable PDF forms:

- Building Representative Notification Form
- Designee Notification Form
- Fire Watch Log

These forms are used by Fire Prevention staff when fire suppression systems are shut off in occupied buildings. The system tracks which properties have active fire watches and enables follow-up on extended impairments.

# **Chapter 8: Civil Service Framework**

## 8.1 Legal Governance

Firefighter hiring at the Denton Fire Department is governed by Texas Local Government Code Chapter 143, which establishes the Civil Service framework for fire departments in cities with populations over 10,000. This framework creates specific requirements for testing, eligibility lists, hiring order, and candidate rights that differ significantly from standard municipal HR processes.

Key implications for the CRR Officer:

- The hiring process operates under Civil Service rules, not standard HR procedures
- NEOGOV hiring workflow is managed independently by the CRR Officer, without dedicated HR support
- Candidate communications, eligibility lists, and hiring decisions must comply with Chapter 143 requirements
- The Civil Service Commission provides oversight and must approve the Position Opportunity Announcement (POA)
- The Meet and Confer Agreement between DFD and the firefighter association may contain additional provisions affecting hiring

## 8.2 Position Details

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Position Title | Firefighter |
| Starting Salary | $86,022 annually + certification pay |
| Salary with Cert Pay | Up to $101,337.60 (depending on certifications held) |
| Age | 18 up to, but not including, 36 (Texas Local Government Code Chapter 143). Never state there is no maximum age. |
| Driver's License | Valid Texas driver's license required |
| Education | High school diploma or GED |
| Certifications (Required) | TCFP Firefighter plus Texas DSHS EMT-Basic or Paramedic, in hand before the conditional offer (usually early April). Candidates may register and test while still in school. |

### Standing Answers for Candidates

These are the answers of record for the recruiting inbox and every published piece:

1. **Denton does not run its own recruit academy.** Candidates arrive already certified.
2. **Certifications are required, not preferred:** TCFP Firefighter plus EMT-Basic or Paramedic, complete before the conditional offer.
3. **Dual-role only.** Every sworn position is firefighter first, EMT-Basic at minimum. Paramedics earn certification pay.
4. **The paramedic exception.** A fully certified paramedic who is hired has fire academy paid for by the department.
5. **Ride-alongs are observation only.** They do not satisfy EMT or paramedic clinical hours; refer students to their school's clinical coordinator.

## 8.3 Preference Points

Under the DFD Civil Service rules and Meet and Confer Agreement, eligible candidates may receive preference points added to their raw exam score:

| Preference | Points | Verification |
|---|---|---|

| Denton Residency | +1 | Proof of Denton address (utility bill, lease, etc.) |

Extra points are verified post-exam by the CRR Officer, added to raw scores, and the adjusted scores are used to generate the certified eligibility list. Send a reminder email about extra points documentation to all registered candidates before exam day.

## 8.4 Tie-Breaking Criteria

When two or more candidates attain the same final score, ties are broken in the following order:

1. Raw score (before preference points)
1. TCFP Structural Firefighter certification
1. EMT-Paramedic (EMT-P) certification or license
1. EMT-Basic (EMT-B) certification
1. Bachelor's degree
1. Earliest registration date

## 9.3 Step 3: Credibility Assessment (VerifEYE)

In FY 25-26, the Denton Fire Department transitioned from traditional polygraph examinations to VerifEYE, a mobile app-based credibility assessment system developed by Converus. VerifEYE uses eye-tracking technology to measure pupil responses to true/false statements, replacing the traditional polygraph examiner and instrument with a phone-based test that takes approximately 10-15 minutes.

### 9.3.1 System Overview

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Vendor | Converus (VerifEYE platform) |
| Test Format | V4R (4 relevant topics) |
| No Admissions Test ID | 3843 (pre-configured) |
| Admissions Test ID | Created by vendor after No Admissions approval |
| Test Delivery | Solo Mode (remote, candidate's phone) or Proctored (in-person with tripod) |
| Initial Setup Cost | $2,728.55 (Converus, charged to 320100.7726) |
| Result Delivery | Via VerifEYE dashboard/portal |

### 9.3.2 The Four Test Topics

The VerifEYE V4R test evaluates credibility across four relevant topics. These topics were reviewed and approved by DFD during initial configuration:

| # | Topic | Definition/Scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ties to Terrorism | Domestic and foreign terrorism; includes specific group examples in test language |
| 2 | Illegal Drug Use (5 yr) | Broad definition; includes marijuana regardless of medical card or state legality |
| 3 | Fired/Resigned in Lieu | Terminated from any job, or resigned to avoid termination |
| 4 | Major Crime | Theft >$300, drug sales, assault, sexual offenses, fraud, arson, gang affiliation, etc. |

### 9.3.3 Two Test Types

VerifEYE provides two distinct test versions. The CRR Officer determines which test to send each candidate based on their Pre-Test Questionnaire responses:

**No Admissions Test (ID 3843):** Used for candidates whose Pre-Test Questionnaire is clean across all four test categories. The credibility test verifies they are being truthful about having no relevant history. This is the baseline test and was approved first.

**Admissions Test:** Used for candidates who disclosed relevant history on their Pre-Test Questionnaire (drug use within the window, termination, criminal activity, etc.). This version is structured to account for their disclosures so the test does not flag things they already reported. The Admissions test was built by the vendor after the No Admissions version was approved.

This is a DFD-branded fillable PDF that serves as the admissions capture tool, replacing the traditional polygraph pre-test interview. ### 9.3.5 VerifEYE Processing Guide

This section covers the end-to-end process for reviewing completed DFD Pre-Test Questionnaires, determining the correct VerifEye exam type (No Admissions vs. Admissions), generating test links through the VerifEye/Converus portal, and sending test link emails to candidates. This process occurs after candidates have passed the Personal History Statement (PHS) stage and submitted their Pre-Test Questionnaires.

**Step 1: Collect Completed Pre-Test Questionnaires**

Distribute the DFD Pre-Test Questionnaire (fillable PDF) to all candidates who passed the PHS stage. Collect completed, signed questionnaires. Verify each form includes:

- Full legal name, date of birth, position, and date
- All sections completed (Sections 1-10)
- Signed and dated Certification on Page 14

**Step 2: Review Each Questionnaire and Determine Exam Type**

The VerifEye system offers two exam types. The correct exam is determined by whether the candidate disclosed relevant history in any of the four test topic categories:

| Category | No Admissions Criteria | Triggers Admissions |
|---|---|---|
| Drug Use | All drug use outside 5-year window OR no drug use at all | Any illegal drug use within the past 5 years |
| Termination | No firings, forced resignations, or asked to resign (Q1-Q3 in Section 3) | Fired, resigned to avoid termination, or asked to resign from any job |
| Criminal Activity | No major criminal disclosures in Section 4 | Major criminal activity disclosed (crimes against persons, arson, burglary, etc.) |
| Terrorism | All No in Section 5 | Any Yes in Section 5 |

Decision Rule: If the candidate has NO admissions across all four categories, assign the No Admissions test. If the candidate disclosed relevant history in any one of the four categories, assign the Admissions test.

**Important Notes on Exam Type Determination:**

- Minor workplace write-ups (e.g., uniform violations, cash register errors, tardiness) do NOT trigger Admissions unless they involved a termination (Q1-Q3)
- Quitting without two weeks' notice (Q8) is NOT a termination category hit
- Marijuana use outside the 5-year window (e.g., last use 2019 when testing in 2026) does NOT trigger Admissions
- Minor petty theft or shoplifting alone does NOT typically trigger Admissions unless combined with other criminal disclosures
- Watch for inconsistencies between sections (e.g., candidate mentions "marijuana arrests" in Section 9 but discloses no criminal history in Section 4). Flag these for the pre-test interview

**Step 3: Generate Test Links in the VerifEye Portal**

1. Log into the VerifEye/Converus Dashboard (see Appendix G.2 for login procedure)
1. Navigate to the VerifEye tab and click "Create Test Link"
1. Select "New Examinee" and enter the candidate's full name and email address
1. Under Test Name, select the appropriate exam type (No Admissions or Admissions) based on your Step 2 review
1. Click Submit. The portal generates a unique URL for that candidate
1. Click "Copy Link" to copy the URL to your clipboard

Important: Each candidate receives their own unique link. Never reuse or share links between candidates.

**Step 4: Email the Test Link to the Candidate**

Send the following email from the CRR Officer's City email address. Paste the VerifEye link after the sign-off at the bottom of the email.

| Email Template |
|---|
| **Subject:** VerifEye Test **To:** *[Candidate email address]* [First Name], Below is your test link. Make sure you follow the instructions completely and listen carefully. The test will ask you various forms of the same question so, if you get one or two messed up, it doesn’t mean you fail. Respectfully, Captain [Last Name] *[PASTE VERIFEYE LINK HERE]* |

The reassuring language about getting one or two questions wrong is intentional and helps reduce candidate anxiety. Do not modify the email template. See also Appendix F.1 for additional template notes.

**Step 5: Track and Monitor Results**

- Results are delivered through the VerifEye dashboard once the candidate completes the test
- Review results: advance passing candidates, DQ failing candidates
- Move passing candidates to the PAT stage in NEOGOV
- Do not notify candidates that they are advancing until the background investigators confirm they have completed their final investigation. Moving candidates in NEOGOV is an internal tracking action, not a candidate notification

## 9.5 Step 4: Physical Ability Test (PAT)

The Physical Ability Test is a timed physical assessment of candidate fitness for firefighting duties. The CRR Officer manages all PAT logistics including equipment procurement, venue coordination, staffing, preparation programs, and results documentation. The PAT is administered per the Denton Fire Department Physical Ability Test Instructor Guide.

### 9.5.1 PAT Course Events

The DFD PAT consists of a stair climb (Event 1) followed by five sequential events (Events 2-6). Candidates wear a 50 lb weighted vest throughout. During Event 1, candidates also wear two 12.5 lb shoulder weights. Event 1 and Events 2-6 are timed separately. Remaining time from Event 1 does not carry over to Events 2-6.

| Event | Time Limit | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Event 1** | 4:00 | Stair Climb: 3-story stairwell to 4th floor landing, 3 times. 50 lb vest + two 12.5 lb shoulder weights. |
| **Rest Period** | 1:30 max | Between Event 1 and Events 2-6. Transfer shoulder weights to proctor; retain 50 lb vest. |
| **Event 2** | n/a | Ladder Raise & Extension: Walk 24ft ladder hand-over-hand, then extend fly section via halyard. |
| **Event 3** | n/a | Equipment Carry: Remove two saws, carry 75ft, 180° turn, carry back, replace in compartment. |
| **Event 4** | n/a | 1-3/4″ Hose Drag: Advance charged hose line, then pull 50ft into marked area from one knee. |
| **Event 5** | n/a | Sledge Swing: Swing sledge in chopping motion to move weighted object ~5 feet. |
| **Event 6** | n/a | Rescue: Drag ~165 lb mannequin 35ft, 180° around drum, 35ft back to finish. |
| **Events 2-6 Total** | **5:30** | Continuous clock across all five events including walking between stations. |

**PPE Note:** Candidates wear department-issued helmets and 50 lb weighted vests. Candidates may bring their own gloves; thin gardening or cotton gloves are not permitted. Only one small vest is available, so the waiting line instructor may need to adjust candidate order based on sizing. If a candidate's helmet falls off during an event, allow them to finish the current event before having them put it back on.

## 12.5 Checkbook Maintenance

### 12.5.1 Requirement

**The CRR Checkbook must be updated on a monthly basis.** This is not optional and it is not a quarterly task. Two things happen when it slips: the officer approves purchases against a balance that is weeks out of date, and the quarterly Program Appraisal budget table gets rebuilt from scratch under deadline instead of read off a current ledger.

The checkbook of record lives on the DFD Public Education page of the HUB, one workbook per fiscal year: [codenton.sharepoint.com/sites/DentonFire/SitePages/DFD-Public-Education.aspx](https://codenton.sharepoint.com/sites/DentonFire/SitePages/DFD-Public-Education.aspx). Open it from the Checkbook tile on that page and save back to the same file. A local copy that only one person can see defeats the purpose. The FY 25-26 checkbook is Community-Risk-Reduction-Checkbook-FY25-26.xlsx.

### 12.5.2 Data Sources

Two systems feed the checkbook, and they do not agree with each other.

**JD Edwards, via the Power BI budget report.** This is the book of record. It shows what actually posted to each account, including purchase-order payments and journal entries that never touch a P-Card. Filter by object code and subledger 2574121 (Recruitment).

Power BI budget report: [City of Denton budget report](https://app.powerbigov.us/groups/me/reports/bc069006-fa9f-4b6c-a8c6-78917d3f224e/ReportSection?ctid=8444bd2b-ab3a-4d6b-bac0-a96fe62e9d2a) (City login required).

**Certify/Emburse Expense Detail Review.** P-Card charges only, but it carries the Reason text that describes what each purchase was for. Use it to populate descriptions, not to determine totals.

The August 2026 reconciliation compared the two side by side for the first time and found them $1,799.32 apart on a $15,401.44 base.

The cause is structural and will recur every year. The Certify Expense Detail Review returns only the CRR Officer's own expense reports. Charges other employees make against these four accounts do not appear in it at all. In FY 25-26 four other employees charged $2,835.32 to the CRR accounts, 16.3% of all P-Card spend on them, including the $1,742.50 Civil Service exam venue at UNT Gateway Center, which was the largest single recruiting expense of the year. Certify alone will understate what has been spent, and the officer approving purchases against it will believe there is money that is already gone.

### 12.5.3 Monthly Procedure

Run this in the first week of the month, after the Emburse P-Card reconciliation deadline has passed.

1. Open the checkbook from SharePoint.
2. Pull JDE charges from the Power BI report for each of the four accounts, October 1 through current. Pull the full fiscal year every month, not just the new month, so that any re-coded transaction corrects itself.
3. Post new lines to the matching account tab starting at the first empty row: date in column B, purchaser in C, vendor in D, description in E, amount in F. Leave column G alone; the running balance formula is already there through row 59.
4. Pull the Certify Expense Detail Review for the same range and use the Reason field to fill in descriptions.
5. Log any purchase order that has been issued but has not posted to the GL in the COMMITTED block at row 62 of the affected tab, with the requisition number in column C. Clear the committed line only when the charge appears in the GL, and enter it in the register at that point. Do not let it sit in both places.
6. Read the Summary tab. Approve future purchases against the Available column, not Remaining.
7. Save back to the SharePoint copy.

### 12.5.4 Watch Items

**October accrual reversals.** Every October the prior September P-Card batch posts twice, once as a JF feed from Certify and once as a JE reversal of the prior year's accrual, both on the same date. In FY 25-26 that was 17 line pairs worth $2,259.09 on documents 910178 and 910249. Match each JE to its JF twin on account, vendor, and amount, and exclude both. They belong to the prior fiscal year.

**Journal entries that are not P-Card feeds.** Any document type other than JF is
somebody moving money. FY 25-26 had a $225.00 EXPENSE RECLASS out of 320200.7764 to
830004.7908 on 02/19/2026 that pulled the IAFC conference registration back out of
Pub Education.

**Subledger coding.** A charge that posts to the right object code without subledger 2574121 will not appear in a subledger-filtered query and will not roll up to recruitment-level reporting. FY 25-26 had two: a $193.00 Facebook charge on 12/20/2025 and a $618.00 Home Depot charge on 06/03/2026.

**Third-party charges.** Check the Emp ID on every GL line. The checkbook register carries the employee ID in the Purchaser column for anyone other than the CRR Officer, so an unfamiliar ID is visible at a glance. Follow up the month a third-party charge posts. These are real commitments against the account and they are invisible in Certify.

The FY 25-26 variance decomposes with nothing left over: $2,835.32 charged by other employees, less $811.00 of the officer's own charges missing the subledger, less the $225.00 conference reclass, equals the $1,799.32 gap.

### 12.5.5 Quarterly and Year-End

At each quarter end, the checkbook Summary tab is the source for the Program Appraisal budget table (see Chapter 14, Inputs/Resources). If the checkbook has been maintained monthly, that table is a copy-paste rather than a rebuild.

The fiscal year closes September 30 and unspent allocation does not carry over. Work backward from September 30, not forward from today, because the binding constraint is procurement lead time rather than the calendar. By the first week of September the practical window has closed for anything requiring three quotes.

## G.2 VerifEYE / Converus: Credibility Assessment

### Overview

VerifEYE is an eye-tracking based credibility assessment that replaced traditional polygraph testing for DFD candidate screening. The system measures involuntary eye behavior changes when a subject is being deceptive.

- **Test Version:** V4R (4 topics)
- **Test ID (No Admissions):** 3843
- **Delivery:** Solo Mode (candidate completes remotely on mobile device) or Proctored (in-person)
- **Dashboard:** Converus admin portal: generates test links, displays results
- **MFA:** Microsoft Authenticator on CRR Officer work phone

### Test Types

- **No Admissions:** Standard test for candidates whose Pre-Test Questionnaire shows no disqualifying admissions ("clean" questionnaire)
- **Admissions:** Used when candidate disclosed issues on the Pre-Test Questionnaire. Verifies the completeness and truthfulness of disclosures.

### Four Test Topics (V4R)

1. Illegal drug use within the past 5 years
2. Employment termination (fired, resigned to avoid firing, or asked to resign)
3. Major criminal activity
4. Terrorism associations

### Workflow

1. Review candidate's Pre-Test Questionnaire responses
2. Determine test type (No Admissions vs. Admissions) based on four trigger categories
3. Log into Converus dashboard (credentials in secure credentials document)
4. Generate test link for appropriate test type
5. Email link to candidate using template (Appendix F.1)
6. Monitor dashboard for completion and results
7. Document results and advance/disqualify in NEOGOV

- **Eligibility list expiration.** Under Civil Service rules, the eligibility list remains valid for a defined period. Track expiration dates and plan subsequent hiring cycles accordingly.
